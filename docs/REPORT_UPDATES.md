## 3. CHAPTER 2 — LITERATURE REVIEW

**Add three references** and a new subsection. These are the most conspicuous
omissions in the current draft:

- **Zhang et al. (2018), "Conversations Gone Awry: Detecting Early Signs of
  Conversational Failure," ACL.** The closest prior work to this project —
  predicting derailment before it occurs, from early conversational signals.
  Frame the difference: they forecast at conversation level, APOLLO-M forecasts
  at community level.
- **Scheffer et al. (2009), "Early-warning signals for critical transitions,"
  Nature 461, 53–59.** Complex systems show measurable warning signs — rising
  variance, critical slowing down — before they tip. This is the theoretical
  grounding for the claim that instability is forecastable at all, and it
  connects directly to the Universe 25 framing in §1.1.
- **Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil et al. (2013), "No Country for Old Members: User
  Lifecycle and Linguistic Change in Online Communities," WWW.** Communities and
  their members evolve measurably over time; supports the churn feature.

> A separate literature asks whether the collapse of a complex system can be

## 4. CHAPTER 3 — DESIGN AND METHODOLOGY

### §3.3.5 Act, Serve and Observe

The moderation recommender is now integrated and produces a graded action per
community. Replace any description of the action mapping as purely deterministic
with:

> Alert bands are derived from the Community Health Index. A trained
> classification model maps the five community features to one of five
> moderation actions, from NO_ACTION to EMERGENCY_INTERVENTION, and is integrated
> into the pipeline. It should be understood as a policy model rather than a
> predictive one: its training labels were generated by a documented threshold
> policy over the same features, so its training accuracy measures agreement with
> that policy and not agreement with observed moderator behaviour. Retraining on
> recorded moderation decisions is the next step for this component.

### §3.3.6 — NEW SUBSECTION: Structural risk from the community graph

> The SNAP hyperlink network is consumed by a GraphSAGE graph neural network
> which produces a structural risk score for each community from its position in
> the inter-community reference graph — 35,776 nodes and 137,821 edges. The
> trained model could not previously be loaded because the module depends on
> torch_geometric; the forward pass was reimplemented directly in PyTorch, since
> mean-aggregation SAGE is expressible as a linear transform of a node's own
> features plus a linear transform of its neighbourhood mean, and the checkpoint
> contains exactly those parameters.
>
> The model was trained with "at risk" defined as a health index below 75, and
> the overwhelming majority of graph nodes carry no health index and therefore
> defaulted to the safe class. The resulting class imbalance compresses every
> output probability into a narrow band near zero. The model still separates
> communities — it produces 15,131 distinct outputs — so the percentile rank
> among analysed communities is published and the raw value retained alongside
> it. Presenting the compressed probability as a calibrated risk would overstate
> what the training data supports. The score is reported alongside the health
> index rather than folded into it, so that the validation figures already
> measured against ground truth remain valid.

**Add after the existing provenance discussion:**

> Live ingestion is operational and is the source of the real corpus. Reddit's
> own API requires credentials issued only to accounts meeting age and activity
> thresholds. Arctic Shift, the community successor to Pushshift, serves
> historical comments and submissions over an open HTTP API with no key, and also
> publishes bulk monthly archives. The adapter normalises its output into the
> same (community, author, text, timestamp) structure that every layer above
> ingestion already consumes, so no component downstream required modification —
> which is the practical demonstration of the platform independence claimed in
> §3.1.
>
> Two properties of the endpoint had to be accommodated. Results are returned
> newest-first relative to an upper time bound, so pagination advances backwards
> through time rather than forwards; and timestamp parameters expressed as dates
> resolve only to a day, so a cursor expressed that way never advances and the
> same page is requested indefinitely. Passing epoch seconds and walking the
> upper bound backwards resolved both, taking collection from fewer than one
> hundred to several thousand comments per community. Because the forecaster
> consumes a daily series, collection samples each day of the window separately
> rather than paging back from the present, which would otherwise return a few
> hours of a single busy community and no time series at all.
>
> Collection runs unattended, re-scanning a rolling window and merging by comment
> identifier so the corpus deepens rather than being replaced.

**Retain the simulation discussion**, and add:

> Both datasets are retained. The benchmark is the only source in which the
> answer is recorded in advance and is therefore the only basis on which recall
> can be measured; it also exercises the clustering and forecasting layers at a
> scale live collection has not yet reached. The real corpus demonstrates that
> the system operates on genuine activity. Both are produced by the same
> pipeline; only the ingestion adapter differs, and the dashboard allows either
> to be selected.

### §3.6 Evaluation Methodology and Results

**Add a subsection — Results on real data:**

> Toxicity measured on real Reddit comments orders communities as an informed
> reader would expect: political and general-news communities score highest and
> r/science lowest. The system was not told this ordering and no part of the
> corpus was constructed to produce it.
>
> The measure that matters most is the spread between communities. On real data
> it is 0.093. On the benchmark corpus, once its sampling bias had been
> corrected, it is 0.016 — approximately six times smaller. This is significant
> because the meso layer rests on the premise that communities differ measurably
> in ways an aggregate can capture; the earlier corpus could not demonstrate that
> premise, and the real corpus does.

> signing key was a literal in the source, which in a public repository is
> sufficient for any reader to mint a valid administrative token; it now comes
